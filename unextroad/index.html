UNextRoad Privacy Policy

Last updated: 11 November 2026

UNextRoad is a free Android app that warns drivers of road closures, lane closures and roadworks ahead on the road, using official UK data. It is published by Eontar Technologies Ltd, United Kingdom ("we"). This page covers only UNextRoad.

1. What we collect and why

Your location. The app asks for permission to use your precise location while you use the app. Your location is used only on your phone to find closures ahead of you: the app downloads fixed, public closure files for the areas shown on the map and compares them with your position on the phone itself. Your position, direction of travel and speed are never sent to us and we do not store them. We keep no location history and build no movement profile. Without location permission the map still works, but the app cannot warn you about closures ahead.

Technical logs. When your phone downloads the closure files and the map, our hosting provider and the map provider receive the usual technical data of any internet request (for example your IP address and the files requested) and may keep standard security logs. The files are fixed, public squares of about 10 km, so the request does not contain your exact position. We do not link these logs to you.

Voice alerts. Spoken warnings use your phone's own text-to-speech engine. We send no audio or text anywhere.

Settings on your phone. Language and the voice on/off choice are saved only on your phone.

Accounts. UNextRoad has no accounts. We collect no name, email address, phone number, contacts, photos or payment data.

Advertising. The free app may show ads from Google AdMob. Ads are switched off during the first period after public launch. When ads are on, Google and its partners may use your device's advertising ID, your IP address and basic device information to show and measure ads. Where the law requires it (UK, EEA, Switzerland), the app asks for your consent first through Google's consent form, and you can change your choice at any time in Settings (shown where Google requires it). You can also reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone settings. How Google uses data from apps that use its services: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites

Data sources. Closure and roadworks information comes from official UK sources, currently National Highways ("Powered by National Highways' Transport Data Feeds"), published under the Open Government Licence v3.0, and more official sources may be added. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors (Open Database Licence).

2. What we do not collect

We do not sell personal data. We do not receive your location. We do not share your location with advertisers: Google receives only the data described under Advertising, and only when ads are on and, where required, you have consented.

3. How long we keep data

Your location is not kept by us (see section 1). Technical logs are kept by our providers for a short period under their own policies. Advertising data is held by Google under its own policies.

4. Your rights

Under UK GDPR and EU GDPR you can ask for access to, correction or deletion of personal data we hold about you, object to processing, and withdraw consent at any time. Because we hold no account and no location history, we normally hold nothing that identifies you. To withdraw ad consent use the privacy option in Settings (where shown) or your phone's advertising ID settings. You may also complain to the UK Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk).

5. Children

UNextRoad is not directed at children under 13 (or under 16 where local law requires) and we do not knowingly collect their data.

6. Important: reference only

Warnings are for reference only. The data comes from third parties and may be incomplete, late, wrong or out of date, and a missing warning does not mean the road is open. Closures can start or end without notice. The driver remains fully responsible for driving and the route; always follow road signs and the road itself, and do not hold or use the phone while driving. To the extent the law allows, we accept no liability for damage caused by relying on the app.

7. Changes

We will update this page and its date when something changes. Material changes will also be announced in the app description.
